A single dial for the whole argument

The One Number

The eighth plate, rebuilt as one instrument. Everything the essay disputes — the markdown, the arch, the gendered wedge, the case for the floor — descends from a single measurable quantity: the elasticity of the labour supply one firm faces. Turn the dial below and watch the entire edifice move at once. Against it sits the one quantity no dial reaches — the competitive wage — which the second plate renders as a ridge in fog.

The competitive labour market is governed, in the end, by one number set silently to infinity: the elasticity of labour supply to the individual firm — how freely workers flow toward it for a little more pay, and away for a little less. Let that number fall to something finite and the firm acquires power over the wage; let it fall further and the company town appears. The economy of the idea is that this same number fixes how much of a worker's product is withheld, where the employment-maximising minimum wage lies, and how far a pay gap can open between identical workers who differ only in how freely they can leave.

So the instrument has one master control, and a handful of linked dials that cannot disagree with it — because they are all reading the same quantity from different angles.

The Markdown, the Arithmetic, the Arch

Three readings of the same dial. The equilibrium that opens the wedge; the fraction it withholds; the policy frontier it bends into an arch — all moving together.

i · The single buyer

ii · The exploitation fraction

iii · The arch — drag the floor

At the far right of the dial — the frictionless limit, ε toward infinity — the supply curve lies flat, marginal cost equals the wage, and the three curves collapse onto the competitive point. Nothing is withheld; the arch has no rising flank left to climb. This is the textbook's world, shown for what the essay claims it is: not the rule, but a single unreachable point. Slide left and it recedes at once. Around ε between five and seven — the band the best studies of how workers actually move between firms keep returning — a tenth to a quarter of the product is already kept, with no single buyer anywhere in sight.

Plate viii · β

The Surface, and the Ridge in Fog

The arch was only a slice. Employment lives on a landscape over two axes — the floor you set, and the elasticity you cannot see. The best floor is a ridge; the fog is the part no instrument locates.

more employment less w* — the crest (unobservable) where each market starts measured ε (5–7)

This is the deepest thing the subject has to say, and the hardest to draw: the number that locates the peak is the one number nobody can measure. The elasticity on the vertical axis is estimated, with error; the competitive wage on the crest is reconstructed, with disagreement. A regulator who would set the floor exactly at the ridge is aiming into the fog — which is why even the friendliest case for a minimum wage is a case for climbing in steps small enough to read the market's answer before the next, feeling for a peak that cannot be seen from below.

NuméraireThe Wage · plate viii · the apparatus

A working instrument for the eighth plate of The Wage. The economics is the standard textbook monopsony under an iso-elastic firm supply curve, normalised so the competitive equilibrium sits at unit wage and unit employment; under that single curve the markdown is exactly one over the elasticity plus one — the essay's arithmetic, made manipulable. Every dial reads the same parameter; the surface is that parameter's policy landscape; the fog is honest about the one quantity the model can never hand over. Set in Fraunces and Newsreader. The argument remains open — now you can turn it.
